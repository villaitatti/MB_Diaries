I Tatti Friday Nov. 28. 1902.
Feeling very sick and dizzy. Christina came to lunch, full of medical fads. Bernhard and Alice took a drive. He finds she has almost no ideas in her pretty head, save those connected with sex. She has a store of rather low improper stories, which aren’t even witty. She reserves them for him - never tells them to me. I had to go to bed, and the doctor, who came to give B.B. his hyper- dermic injection of phosphate, pronounced me a victim to the now fashionable complaint from which Alys, Christina, Mother and B.B. are all suffering - Uric acid! By it he explains my frequent colds. At present I have a slight liver upset, it is nothing, but the Uric Acid is serious, for it demands what I do hate, exercise, and for all the rest of my life!
