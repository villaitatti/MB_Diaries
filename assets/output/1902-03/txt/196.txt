Sunday May 10. 1903. I Tatti Settignano
Jephson too ill to talk of himself and his plans until nearly midnight, when all his worries came out in a burst. I can’t enter into them here, but his American fiancée seems to be terribly selfish and inconsiderate and he is in a miserable, upset condition, without a penny of money.
Some Americans, Miss Mitchell and Miss Phillips came to lunch, and a painter - who makes splendid copies - Miss Williams, called. Bernhard and I had a drive, and called on Mrs. Thorold who said that innumerable abbots and priests had thanked her for taking Algar out of the sacred profession. She said he had been engaged 22 times before he was engaged to her, and that he only proposed to her because he thought she wouldn’t let him kiss her without - wherein, she said, he was quite mistaken!!
