Sunday May 3. 1903.
Beautiful weather again. Bernhard better, Mr. and Mrs. Jack Chapman came in the morning. He is the eccentric but delightful man who burnt off his hand - no one knows why. Now he goes on crutches, and is awfully delicate. She is also lame, but very distinguished looking, with ideas of her own. Her friend, Mrs. Lyttelton, came to lunch. It was agreeable without being exciting. They were not really “in our world”. Mrs. May Morris who came with the Houghtons to tea belongs there much more, quiet as she is. Douglas Ainslie arrived in the evening, a boring, self-centred man, only saved by being interested in hearing stories about people.
I began Mrs. Ady’s “Isabella d’Este”
