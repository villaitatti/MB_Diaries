President Seelye introduced me as a living example” of what Smith College could do for women. I spoke to them about 45 minutes, and it was easy and appeared to be interesting. I thought it might poor, but they said that they had never (since Mother) had a woman who spoke with such ease and naturalness. Afterwards we had supper with Mrs. Rosseter, the Matron of the Dewey House. Lunched with Prof. Gardiner.

Friday Oct. 30. 1903. Smith College

Mrs. Berenson came across yesterday - another rather depressing person, about whose life I can’t feel anything but hopeless. Senda and Bessie are miserable here, and so are most of the other women teachers I have met.
