So we crawled back to Hampton and had dinner there, and put in our time waiting for the train B.B. resting, and Tyler and I walking through the cemetery discussing the difficult problem of Gertrude Moulton, his sister. And then we came back in the train all together and went to the Union Club, where Mr. Tyler gave us a “plain supper” as he called it:
Caviare 
Raw Oysters 
Terrapin 
Game, jelly and salad
