<Hotel Webster, New York> Saturday, Jan. 2, 1909
We lunched with Bessie Marbury and Elsie de Wolfe. Bessie was talking about two young men from California who have come to New York to seek their fortunes. One came before the other, and explained that he had got a position as art editor for a paper, but that his friend, Anthony wasn’t engaged on anything, but meant to be a  writer  of plays. “Anthony has money,  but you mustn’t let that prejudice you against him ,  Miss Marbury!” the boy explained with great insistence.
She used several amusing expressions about getting Rooseveldt [sic] to speak at the Colony Club  –s aid he was “gun shy” of New York (like a dog once shot in hunting) and that he had “cold feet” about New York.
