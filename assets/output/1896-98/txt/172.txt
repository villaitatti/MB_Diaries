Then we had a lugubrious tea, and all filed off to Miss Paget’s study. She had put her papers etc. together very well, and I must say she convinced me that she had been  on the track  for many years. Her manner was well-bred and courteous, with a touch of geniality and humour that I liked. The other three never smiled once, nor unbent from a portentous fragility. The climax of funniness – evidently well prepared for, and led up to skilfully, was the last extract from Miss Paget’s notes that she read us – which she said was the only thing ever suggested by Mr. Berenson that she had ever used in print.
