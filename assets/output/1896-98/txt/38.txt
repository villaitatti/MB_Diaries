She was full of the idea that most novels become exhausted before they finish, and need  replenishment  by way of further unexpected developments, which most authors do not know how to give. She was running down “The Marble Faun”, as not in tune with the historical time it is laid in. But she talked well, with incisive phrases, and was cordial and distinguished in manner. She offered to teach me singing. 
Bernhard finished his article on the Raphael cartoon of the British Museum and I printed it to send off. Maud Cruttwell went, much to our relief. We breathe again! 
Sunday Nov. 29. 1896. Villa Rosa. Fiesole
A dreadful afternoon - first the little Morgan boys came - but they were nice - but the dreadful thing was an interminable call from Miss Dibblee. Bernhard went to dine with Mr. Davis, who is just starting for his Morris furnished  dahabia  (?) (sic) with £100 worth of flags, £500 plated silver, 30 large trunks, a manservant and a maidservant. He told Bernhard to invest all his money in sugar.
