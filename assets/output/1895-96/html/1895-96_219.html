Placci keeps up his spirits wonderfully - and ours too, [0218] I must say, for Bernhard seems so nervous and cross that unless he had told me out and out that it was not so, I should not be able to help thinking that he dislikes me intensely. Everything I do and say gets on his nerves, whenever he looks at me it is to tell me I am fat, or red, or hairy, or slouchy, or untidy, and these remarks he makes and others on my Americanisms and my general stupidity in bitter tones with a frown and not the slightest appearance of finding anything nice in me. It may be one way of expressing affection, to be greatly concerned with another person’s faults and defects, but it is not the happiest way. It makes me very unhappy, and again and again these last two days has given me hours of such acute unhappiness that I have felt like going off and travelling by myself, where I should not be constantly humiliated by looks of disgust and disapproval in tones of contempt.
