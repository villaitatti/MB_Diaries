week. Pazienza!
I had my music-lesson, and came up to meet Lina and Mr. and Miss Cust at lunch. Miss Cust is very pleasant, but she is dissatisfied and unhappy, poor thing, growing old (she is 30) with no particular bough to perch upon. Cust is killing  –  one of these naïve Englishmen who tells you his income and his family history, and his breach-of-promise case in the first breath! They came to the Tatti with us, and then we all had tea at the Mortons. Afterwards Logan and I called at Poggio, and I had a long chat with dear Mr. Ross. 
I often wonder what Evalyne is feeling now.
Saturday Nov. 3. 1900.
Worked in the morning. Went to town and priced the lace. They said it was worth £1000 to the trade but that no ‘particolare’ could get it for less than £3000!
