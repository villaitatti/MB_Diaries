Thursday May 2. 1901.

Percy Fielding came up to lunch. He had been traveling with Aubrey Waterfield, and they finally had a quarrel, in the course of which Percy told his companion that I had said he was the most selfish person who ever stayed in my house! It was quite true, but I did not say it to be repeated!! I paid various calls and then met B.B. at the Mortons, a goodbye visit. Stickney came to dinner and was interesting and sympathetic, though he praised Anatole France rather too lightly. But he is one of the most interesting people, more or less in our line, that we know. Poor Jephson telegraphed that his bad news was confirmed. He added that my letters were a consolation to him. I thought they would be, as he is terribly alone in his trouble. He likes to boast, or to talk of Miss Head, but hates to speak of his real anxieties – health and money.
