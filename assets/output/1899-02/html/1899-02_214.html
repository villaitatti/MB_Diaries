Tuesday January 21. 1902 I Tatti

We lunched at the Countess Serristori’s, Placci being the other guest, and the Count happily away. She is a brilliant, perfectly intelligent woman, and talks well. Subtle, appreciative, interested, observing. Afterwards Bernhard went to the Uffizi, and I wrung my soul ‘philanthropising’. I do loathe it, but it seems forced on me. We called on the Rosses after tea, and the old man delighted us with vivid stories of the Great Ambassador, Lord Bradford de Radcliffe.

Wednesday January 22. 1902

Bernhard went with Dr. Grazzini to see a “Botticelli” and a “Donatello”- the usual inganno – Dr. G. about as intelligent in the matter as BB would be in diagnosing a disease. Stein came early in the afternoon, and we sat for an hour in the warm sunshine. He was not actually unpleasant, that is all I can say. He remained to dinner. I am absorbed in Carlyle’s Life.
